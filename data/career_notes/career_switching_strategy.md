# General Career Switching Strategy

## Assess transferable skills first
- Before assuming you need to start from zero, identify skills from your current field that transfer: communication, project management, domain expertise, data handling, stakeholder management.
- Many career switches succeed by combining an existing domain (e.g. healthcare, finance, retail) with a new technical skill, rather than competing purely on technical skill against candidates with years more experience.

## Build proof, not just claims
- A portfolio, a small project, a certificate with a real deliverable, or contributions to something public are stronger signals than "interested in X" on a resume.
- Employers hiring career switchers weigh demonstrated initiative heavily, since it substitutes for the work-history signal a traditional candidate would have.

## Target the right entry point
- Look for roles explicitly labeled "associate," "junior," or that list 0-2 years experience — don't apply broadly to mid/senior roles expecting the switch itself to be forgiven.
- Some companies have formal career-switcher or apprenticeship programs — these are worth specifically searching for.

## Timeline expectations
- A realistic career switch (learning + job search) commonly takes 4-9 months of consistent effort, depending on the size of the skill gap and how much time can be devoted weekly.
- Networking (informational interviews, alumni networks, online communities) meaningfully shortens this timeline compared to cold applications alone.

## Common mistakes
- Trying to learn too many new skills at once instead of going deep on the 2-3 skills most requested in target job postings.
- Waiting to apply until feeling "fully ready" — applying while still building skills, and being honest about where you are, is usually more effective than an indefinite delay.
