# Technical Interview Preparation

## Before the interview
- Research the company's product, recent news, and the specific team if known.
- Prepare 2-3 stories using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) covering: a conflict you resolved, a project you're proud of, a mistake you learned from.
- Review the job description line by line and be ready to map your experience to each requirement.

## During a technical/coding interview
- Think out loud — interviewers evaluate your reasoning process, not just the final answer.
- Clarify requirements and edge cases before writing code, rather than assuming.
- If stuck, state your current understanding and what you'd try next — silence is worse than visible (if imperfect) reasoning.
- Test your solution against at least one example before declaring it done.

## During a behavioral interview
- Be specific — avoid vague claims like "I'm a team player" without a concrete example backing it up.
- It's fine to acknowledge a real weakness or past mistake, as long as you show what you learned or changed afterward.

## Questions to ask the interviewer
- Asking thoughtful questions about the team's current challenges signals genuine interest.
- Good areas: what a typical week looks like, how the team measures success, what's changed on the team in the last year.

## Common mistakes
- Memorizing answers verbatim instead of understanding the underlying story — this reads as rehearsed and falls apart under follow-up questions.
- Not asking any questions at the end — this can read as disinterest.
