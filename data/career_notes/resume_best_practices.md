# Resume Writing Best Practices

## Structure
- Keep entry-level and mid-level resumes to one page.
- Order sections by relevance: for career switchers, put a skills section near the top; for experienced candidates, lead with a work history section.
- Use reverse chronological order within each section (most recent first).

## Bullet points
- Start each bullet with an action verb (built, led, reduced, automated, designed).
- Quantify impact wherever possible — "reduced page load time by 40%" beats "improved performance."
- Avoid restating the job title as a bullet ("Responsible for X") — describe what was actually done and its outcome.
- Each bullet should answer: what did you do, how did you do it, what was the measurable result?

## Tailoring for a specific job
- Mirror the exact terminology used in the job posting where it's honestly applicable (e.g. if the posting says "stakeholder management" and you did that, use that exact phrase).
- Reorder or re-emphasize existing bullets to match what the target job cares about most — don't fabricate new experience.
- A tailored resume for a specific role consistently outperforms a single generic resume sent everywhere.

## Common mistakes
- Objective statements that are generic and could apply to any job ("Seeking a challenging role where I can grow").
- Dense paragraphs instead of scannable bullet points — recruiters spend seconds per resume on a first pass.
- Listing skills with no evidence of them being used anywhere in the experience section.
